# LIVE RESEARCH PLAYBOOK

## Purpose

Provide a repeatable live-research procedure for deciding what to create now.

## Before every new production session

1. Get the current date.
2. Open Adobe's current Generative AI guidelines.
3. Open Adobe's current photo technical requirements.
4. Open current refusal/moderation guidance.
5. Open current distinct-content guidance.
6. Open current title/keyword guidance.
7. Open current Adobe trend/collection pages.
8. Check current Premium Collection guidance when the concept could qualify.
9. Search for current relevant industry/customer signals only after reading Adobe's own material.

## Search objectives

Research five separate questions:

### A. What themes are being signaled now?

Use current Adobe trend collections, cultural insights, missions and Premium Collection themes.

### B. What buyer problem can be served?

Translate themes into a concrete buyer use case: ad, website, campaign, article layout, presentation, app, wellness, technology, sustainability, travel, workplace, accessibility, etc.

### C. How crowded is the obvious execution?

Inspect current Adobe Stock search/category results when available. Look for repeated generic compositions. Do not copy another contributor. The goal is to detect saturation and find a gap.

### D. What can generative AI do unusually well?

Prefer concepts that benefit from controlled synthetic production: difficult environments, precise conceptual scenes, generic products, complex but non-proprietary compositions, or visual metaphors that remain commercially usable.

### E. What can generative AI still do poorly?

Down-rank concepts likely to fail anatomy, text, complex machinery, exact branding, precise current events, highly specific real-world architecture, or any other detail that creates high QC/legal risk.

## Output of research

Produce:

`SIGNAL -> EVIDENCE -> BUYER -> GAP -> CONCEPT -> RISK -> DECISION`

For each candidate identify source URLs and whether the claim is verified evidence or a hypothesis.

## No fabricated metrics

Do not invent:

- sales counts;
- download counts;
- buyer conversion rates;
- approval probabilities;
- competitor earnings;
- “most searched” rankings.

When a metric is not public, label it `UNKNOWN`.

## Research freshness

A dated snapshot may be retained in `research/`, but live research outranks snapshots. Update historical snapshots rather than silently treating them as current.
