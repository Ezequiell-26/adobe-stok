# Market Value & Buyer Demand — Adobe Stock

## Mission

Choose assets because they solve real visual communication needs, not because they merely look impressive.

The target is **licensable commercial value**: an image that a real buyer can use with minimal adaptation and that is discoverable through truthful metadata.

## Buyer-first model

For every candidate asset, identify:

- buyer persona;
- industry/use case;
- communication message;
- likely placement;
- useful orientation/aspect ratio;
- required copy space;
- whether people are necessary;
- why a buyer would license this rather than use a generic alternative.

## High-value characteristics

Favor assets with several of these properties when appropriate:

- clear concept readable at thumbnail size;
- broad but specific commercial utility;
- authentic contemporary context;
- multiple potential industries;
- adaptable composition;
- clean negative space;
- realistic human behavior when people are included;
- credible products/materials/environments;
- strong emotional or functional communication;
- distinct visual point of view;
- timeless core concept with a current execution.

## Demand is not the same as competition

High interest can coexist with extreme saturation. The agent must model both.

Prefer:

`DEMAND SIGNAL × BUYER UTILITY × DIFFERENTIATION × QUALITY CEILING`

over:

`POPULAR KEYWORD × GENERIC IMAGE`

## Concept portfolio matrix

Before generation, attempt to identify:

### A. Core commercial need
A concept that solves a broad, recurring visual need.

### B. Current cultural/business signal
A current issue, behavior, audience or trend with credible evidence.

### C. Underserved intersection
A more specific combination of audience, situation and use case that may be less saturated.

### D. Evergreen backup
A durable concept that remains useful even if current trends change.

The preferred asset often combines A+B or A+C rather than relying exclusively on a short trend.

## Search intent

Metadata must reflect what a buyer actually sees, not what the creator wishes the image represented.

Build keyword groups:

1. primary subject;
2. action/state;
3. environment;
4. audience/demographic when visibly relevant;
5. concept/business message;
6. context/industry only when visually supported;
7. composition/use-related descriptors when accurate.

Do not use hidden concepts, speculative SEO, competitor names, artists, brands, celebrities or unrelated trend words.

## Commercial design utility

Evaluate:

- left/right/center copy space;
- subject placement;
- crop resilience;
- safe areas for headlines;
- horizontal and vertical variants only when they create materially different value;
- foreground/background separation;
- visual hierarchy;
- whether a designer can place text without covering the essential concept.

## Real-world value test

Before approving a concept, ask:

> "Could a marketing team, publisher, startup, agency, educator, health organization, retailer, travel company or other legitimate buyer use this image in a real project within minutes?"

If the answer is unclear, redesign the concept.

## Competitive advantage doctrine

The agent must not try to "beat competitors" through volume, manipulation or imitation.

It should compete through:

- better concept selection;
- stronger art direction;
- more believable photography;
- greater usefulness;
- stronger differentiation;
- cleaner metadata;
- disciplined curation;
- lower avoidable refusal risk.

Adobe's own guidance emphasizes commercially relevant, unique, high-quality generative AI content and warns against overly similar submissions. Use that as the operating principle.

## No fake certainty

Never output:

- guaranteed buyer demand;
- guaranteed sales;
- guaranteed ranking;
- guaranteed approval;
- fake market-share estimates.

Instead report:

`EVIDENCE → INTERPRETATION → COMMERCIAL HYPOTHESIS → UNCERTAINTY`

## Final buyer-value gate

BLOCK or redesign if the concept is:

- visually impressive but commercially vague;
- dependent on a fleeting meme with no buyer use case;
- generic in a heavily saturated topic without meaningful differentiation;
- difficult to search truthfully;
- legally risky;
- technically unreliable;
- too similar to assets already considered/submitted.
