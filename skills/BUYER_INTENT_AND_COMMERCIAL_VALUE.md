# BUYER INTENT AND COMMERCIAL VALUE

## Purpose

A stock asset is valuable when a buyer can immediately imagine a real commercial use for it. Evaluate concepts from the buyer's side before optimizing aesthetics.

## Buyer-use-case matrix

For every candidate concept, identify 3–6 plausible uses, such as:

- advertising campaign;
- website hero/banner;
- social media creative;
- editorial-layout-like commercial design;
- presentation or report;
- app/product marketing;
- wellness/health communication;
- technology/business communication;
- travel/hospitality marketing;
- packaging or generic product mockup context.

Do not claim an editorial use if the asset is being represented as authentic news documentation.

## Commercial-use test

Ask:

1. What problem does this image solve for a designer or marketer?
2. What message can the buyer communicate with it?
3. Does the image leave practical copy space where useful?
4. Can the buyer crop it without destroying the concept?
5. Is the subject immediately understandable at thumbnail size?
6. Does the image feel premium enough for professional advertising?
7. Is the context specific enough to feel intentional but generic enough to license broadly?

If the answer to most questions is no, redesign the concept before generation.

## Value multipliers

Prefer concepts with several simultaneous properties:

- clear message;
- strong focal point;
- believable contemporary context;
- flexible composition;
- negative space;
- culturally aware but non-IP-specific context;
- realistic production quality;
- uncommon but useful interpretation.

Avoid novelty that reduces buyer utility.

## Buyer-language metadata

Write title and keywords in terms buyers actually need to search for the visible concept. Do not add abstract marketing language that is not visually supported.
