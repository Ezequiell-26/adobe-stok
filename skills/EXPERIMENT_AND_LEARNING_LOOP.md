# EXPERIMENT AND LEARNING LOOP

## Purpose

Turn each submission outcome into structured evidence without pretending that one result proves a market rule.

## Record per asset

- date;
- concept family;
- demand signals used;
- buyer use cases;
- generation model/version;
- prompt version;
- major settings;
- export specifications;
- QC failures and fixes;
- legal/license checks;
- metadata;
- Adobe outcome;
- refusal reason if provided;
- licensing/performance data when actually available from the contributor account;
- lesson learned.

## Evidence levels

### A — verified platform evidence
Official Adobe rule, trend, mission, collection or portal evidence.

### B — account performance evidence
Actual contributor data such as approved/refused assets or observed licensing activity, when available to the owner.

### C — market hypothesis
A reasoned hypothesis based on current market signals but not proven by account data.

Never upgrade C to A or B without evidence.

## Iteration rule

When an asset is refused, first map the reason to a gate. Fix the root problem before resubmission. If the reason is similarity, redesign the concept rather than merely changing color or crop.

## Portfolio intelligence

Maintain a rolling list of:

- overused generic subjects to approach cautiously;
- demand signals worth exploring;
- successful concepts with evidence;
- recurring QC failures;
- recurring legal/IP risks;
- concepts already covered by the portfolio.

Do not assume a refused asset is commercially worthless; distinguish moderation failure from market failure.

## Update discipline

Current web research should refresh volatile information. Stable technical rules can remain in the repository but must still be checked against official Adobe pages before high-volume work.
