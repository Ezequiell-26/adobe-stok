# SKILL — STOCK DEMAND RESEARCH

## Objective

Select what to create based on **current evidence**, commercial utility and differentiation. Never pretend to know exact Adobe Stock sales demand unless a reliable current source provides it.

## Research hierarchy

### A. Official Adobe signals — highest priority

Check the current Adobe Stock trend/collections pages and contributor guidance.

Record:

- current named creative trends;
- current cultural insight collections;
- recurring themes Adobe is actively curating;
- any new contributor guidance that affects subject selection.

### B. Commercial-use signals

Look for current buyer-facing themes across Adobe's search/category/collection surfaces when publicly observable. Evaluate recurring commercial use cases such as:

- advertising campaigns;
- websites and landing pages;
- social media campaigns;
- workplace communications;
- wellness and fitness;
- technology adoption;
- sustainability;
- family/multigenerational content;
- accessibility;
- food and lifestyle;
- local culture;
- education;
- finance/business concepts.

Treat visible marketplace frequency as a **signal**, not a precise sales metric.

### C. Saturation analysis

Search Adobe Stock for the intended concept when possible. Ask:

- Is the result set dominated by nearly identical generic AI imagery?
- Are the same poses/compositions repeated?
- Is there room for a materially different commercial concept?
- Can the idea be made more specific without adding IP risk?

High saturation does not automatically mean “never create”. It means the concept must earn differentiation.

## Scoring model

Score each candidate from 0–5 on:

`Trend evidence`
`Buyer usefulness`
`Differentiation opportunity`
`Visual clarity`
`Production feasibility`
`IP/release safety`
`Portfolio expansion value`

Then apply:

`TOTAL = 7 dimensions`

Do not publish a fake precise probability of sale or approval. The score is a **planning heuristic**, not a forecast.

## Concept selection rule

Select the candidate with the strongest evidence-to-risk ratio and a clear buyer use case.

Prefer a concept that:

- can be understood immediately;
- is specific enough to avoid generic AI sameness;
- has one primary subject;
- supports useful negative space/crops;
- does not require brands or celebrities;
- can be rendered photorealistically;
- can produce multiple materially different future concepts without spam.

## Current 2026 baseline

At the repository reference date, Adobe's trend collections highlight:

- sensory appeal;
- human connection;
- playful surrealism;
- authentic local cultures/communities.

Adobe's cultural insight selections also highlight areas such as social fitness for Gen Z, analog low-tech lifestyles, fandom/community, women/wellness/longevity, Gen Z in the workplace, multigenerational messaging, accessibility, sustainability, Generation Alpha and contemporary men/masculinity.

These are the **starting signals only**. Refresh them on every research run.

## Required research output

Return:

`TOPIC`
`EVIDENCE`
`BUYER USE CASE`
`SATURATION RISK`
`IP/RELEASE RISK`
`DIFFERENTIATION ANGLE`
`PROPOSED CONCEPT`
`WHY THIS CONCEPT`

## What NOT to do

Do not write:

- “This is guaranteed to sell.”
- “Adobe asks for this the most.”
- “This is the #1 niche.”
- “This will definitely be approved.”

unless the exact statement is directly supported by current reliable evidence.
