# DEMAND EVIDENCE MATRIX

## Goal
Select stock concepts using current evidence rather than intuition alone.

## Evidence buckets

### A. Direct Adobe demand signals
- active Adobe Missions and content briefs;
- official Adobe trend collections;
- Adobe customer-facing collections;
- official contributor guidance about customer needs;
- Premium Collection guidance.

### B. Customer/use-case signals
- advertising campaign patterns;
- recurring business communication needs;
- seasonal commercial planning;
- industries with obvious visual-content requirements;
- underserved cultural/geographic contexts.

### C. Search/discovery signals
Use current search results only as directional evidence. Search visibility is not the same as purchase volume.

### D. Portfolio evidence
When the user provides personal analytics:
- downloads;
- licenses;
- revenue;
- approval/refusal rates;
- keyword performance;
- category performance.

Personal evidence outranks generic assumptions about that contributor's portfolio, but does not automatically generalize to the entire marketplace.

## Opportunity labels

`HIGH_EVIDENCE` = multiple strong current signals.
`MEDIUM_EVIDENCE` = one strong signal plus supporting evidence.
`LOW_EVIDENCE` = mostly indirect signals.
`UNKNOWN` = insufficient evidence.

## Required output
For every selected concept record:
- demand evidence;
- customer/use cases;
- competitive saturation proxy;
- differentiation opportunity;
- timing;
- evidence confidence;
- reasons for rejection of alternatives.

## Hard rule
Never write:
- "most searched";
- "most sold";
- "guaranteed seller";
- "will be selected";
- "higher sales probability"
unless direct, current, verifiable data actually supports the exact claim.

Prefer:
- "strong current demand signal";
- "commercially relevant hypothesis";
- "underserved opportunity based on available evidence";
- "evidence confidence: high/medium/low".
