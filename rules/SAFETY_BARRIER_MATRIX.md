# Safety Barrier Matrix — Adobe Stock

Every asset must pass all applicable barriers. A later barrier cannot override an earlier failure.

## B0 — Source Integrity

- Current official Adobe policy checked when web access exists.
- Current date recorded.
- Current generation-tool terms checked.
- No fabricated market statistics.
- No fabricated Adobe requirements.

FAIL: stop research and refresh sources.

## B1 — Concept Safety

BLOCK:
- protected characters;
- celebrity/public-figure likenesses without rights;
- real identifiable people without appropriate rights/releases when required;
- recognizable protected artwork;
- distinctive third-party products/packaging/designs as the subject without clearance;
- real news/event simulation presented as authentic documentation;
- deceptive or harmful concepts that violate Adobe policies.

## B2 — Prompt Safety

BLOCK third-party artist names, brands, product names, characters, agencies, government entities, or copyrighted-work references used as style/SEO bait.

The prompt must describe the intended visual result directly.

## B3 — Generation Integrity

Reject outputs with:
- malformed anatomy;
- inconsistent identity/model features;
- impossible geometry;
- impossible reflections;
- impossible shadows;
- inconsistent lighting;
- duplicated/fused objects;
- warped text or pseudo-text;
- plastic/melted materials;
- render seams, halos or obvious AI artifacts.

## B4 — Technical Integrity

For photo submission, verify the current Adobe requirement at the time of upload. Current repository baseline is:

- JPEG;
- sRGB;
- 4–100 MP;
- <=45 MB;
- sharp and correctly exposed.

Do not confuse megabytes with megapixels.

## B5 — Commercial Integrity

The asset must have a clear buyer/use case and genuine stock utility.

BLOCK an image that is merely decorative, conceptually vague, unusable for practical design, or overwhelmingly generic relative to its competition.

## B6 — Metadata Integrity

- title truthful;
- keywords visually supported;
- most important terms first;
- no keyword stuffing;
- no third-party IP names used for search manipulation;
- category appropriate;
- AI disclosure accurate.

## B7 — Similarity / Spam Barrier

Before upload, compare against the contributor's own planned/submitted series.

Do not submit a batch whose only changes are:
- tiny crop;
- filter;
- color shift;
- rotation/flip;
- small zoom;
- minor pose change;
- insignificant background change.

Prefer concept-level or meaningful use-case diversification.

Adobe currently states that no more than three iterations of similar assets should be submitted and warns that excessive similarity/spam can lead to rejection or account action. Always verify the current official wording before bulk submission.

## B8 — Release / License Barrier

HOLD when:
- model-release status is unclear;
- property-release status is unclear where required;
- tool license for commercial stock use is not evidenced;
- provenance or rights cannot be documented.

Never infer rights from the fact that an AI generator technically produced the image.

## B9 — Final Human-Like Review

The agent must challenge its own asset:

- Is every important object physically plausible?
- Would a professional photographer accept the lighting?
- Would a designer find the composition useful?
- Is the image still strong at thumbnail size?
- Is it clean at 100%?
- Is the concept meaningfully differentiated?
- Are the metadata claims literally visible?

Any material doubt = BLOCK/HOLD.

## Barrier philosophy

The system is deliberately conservative. It is better to reject a weak asset before upload than to manufacture confidence around a questionable one.

No barrier can guarantee Adobe approval because Adobe's moderation decision is external to this repository.
